# AMENDMENT v1.2 to adaptive-phases-and-schedule-v1 — owner-directed

A. One pain record, everywhere. Any body-part pain selection anywhere in the app — the physio screens, the daily check-in, "Something hurts", "Something's off" on a card — writes the SAME timeline entry, with the same tag, body part, severity and date. One record, one source of truth, read by the scheduler, the governors, the watchdog and the pattern report. Reporting the same pain twice updates the existing entry instead of creating a second one. Add a test: the same pain entered from three different screens produces one entry and one plan change.

B. One season state drives the whole day. The athlete's single season state and phase drive everything: every prescription in Hammer's Today, the before-you-start drawer, the weekly card, lifting, throwing, hitting, speed, conditioning, arm care and recovery. One season state per athlete at a time. No screen may show or use another. Add a test that samples every card an athlete receives in a day and confirms all of them carry the same season state and phase.

C. The offseason arc must match the days available. Do not simply scale one shape. Treat different lengths as different jobs, measured in days: 140 days or more (20+ weeks) → the full arc, with room for a true absorb block and a full explosiveness block. 98 to 139 days (14 to 19 weeks) → full arc, compressed; absorb and explosiveness keep their minimums, sport ramp shortens. 56 to 97 days (8 to 13 weeks) → three phases only, sport ramp folded into the sharpening week. 28 to 55 days (4 to 7 weeks) → two phases by priority need, then the re-entry ramp. Under 28 days → bridge mode: capacity, then sharpen, then ramp. So 168 days and 126 days produce visibly different plans, not the same plan stretched. Show the day count and the phase lengths in the owner's staff view.

D. Arm readiness — the highest standard in the app. Nothing we prescribe may create an arm problem, and no athlete may arrive at a season with a body ready to throw hard and an arm that is not. Case by case: every break and every ramp is computed from that athlete's age, position, last season's innings and pitch counts, pain history, current arm tank level, velocity trend, and how they tolerated previous ramps. Break rules: a break is scheduled only when it fits — the ramp that follows it must finish at least 7 days before the next known game; if the calendar cannot fit both, shorten the break, never shorten the ramp; keep the annual rest guidance already in the app for each age; rest from throwing is never zero for a young arm. Ramp length, from the break length: ≤7 days → ramp 3 to 5 days; 8 to 21 days → ramp equals the break, minimum 7 days; 22 to 56 days → ramp 1.5 times the break, minimum 21 days, minimum 28 for pitchers; more than 56 days → 42 to 56 days for pitchers, 28 for position players. Inside the ramp: change one thing at a time — distance, then volume, then intent; never two in the same step. Intent climbs in steps (about 60, 70, 80, 90, then full), and full intent only after full distance with no pain flags. Each step needs its gate: pain-free 48 hours after the session, next-day soreness under threshold, arm tank below its line, and the previous step's volume completed. A failed gate repeats the step; it never skips ahead. High-intent work (bullpens at max, pulldowns, velocity days) unlocks only after the full ramp plus two clean weeks at high intent. Pitch Smart caps are unchanged. Velocity must be earned by the arm, not just the body: if strength, speed or power markers rise faster than arm capacity markers, the app holds high-intent throwing and keeps building capacity first, and says so in plain words. Fascia-first lens: arm work is whole chain — hips and trunk deliver the force, the springy tissue of the arm stores and returns it, and the cuff, scap, forearm and wrist chain handle deceleration. Capacity and deceleration work run year-round, never only in the offseason.

E. Where the explanation lives. The phase explanation belongs in the before-you-start drop-down. At the top: the phase name, weeks left, and what comes next. Under it, a single collapsed line: "Why this phase matters." Tapping it opens two or three sentences in plain words. One tap to open, one to close, nothing else added to the drawer. No clutter, no jargon, no block numbers.

F. Tests to add: one pain entry from three screens equals one record and one plan change; every card in a day carries the same season state and phase; a 168-day and a 126-day offseason produce different phase lengths and different content, not the same plan stretched; a 30-day throwing break produces a ramp of at least 45 days for a pitcher, and the ramp ends at least 7 days before the first game; if the calendar cannot fit break plus ramp, the break shortens and the ramp stays whole; a failed ramp gate repeats the step instead of advancing; high-intent throwing stays locked until the ramp is complete and two clean weeks have passed; the drawer shows the phase explanation in one tap and adds nothing else.
