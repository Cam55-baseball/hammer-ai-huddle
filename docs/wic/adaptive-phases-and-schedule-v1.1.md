# Adaptive Phases v1.1 — Amendment (owner, 2026-09-24)

## A. One season state, total unity
- Each athlete has ONE season state (offseason, pre-season, in-season, post-season) and ONE phase at a time. Lifting, throwing, speed and bat speed all follow it. This replaces the per-discipline phase tracks in v1 §3.
- Only the CONTENT differs by discipline. The rules, the phase and the season state never differ.
- The one exception is a HOLD: pain or injury can hold a single discipline in reduced work. It is flagged as a hold with its reason, never shown as a different phase.
- Add an invariant: no two disciplines may sit in different phases unless a hold is active.

## B. A flowing season stays a season
Never treat the days between games as offseason. Measured on the gap between game days:
- **Gap of 14 days or less:** in-season the whole way through. Weekend-only play means the whole week is in-season training, not an offseason block.
- **Gap of 15 to 27 days:** stay in-season, but insert a two-week mini block (Power Building or Explosiveness, whichever the athlete needs most), followed by a re-entry ramp of at least 5 days before the next game.
- **Gap of 28 days or more:** treat as a short offseason window using the v1 §4 maths, followed by a re-entry ramp of at least 7 days.
- **No play for 6 weeks or more (break, injury, postponement):** a full arc is allowed, followed by a re-entry ramp of 10 to 14 days.

## C. The re-entry ramp — safe onboarding into explosive play
Never let an athlete go from mid-offseason work straight into game intensity.
- The ramp always runs before the first game back and never overlaps a game.
- It builds sport intent in steps: elastic primers, then max-velocity exposure, then sport-speed throwing and swinging, ending with a game-ready day.
- The ramp is never skipped or compressed below its minimum. If the calendar leaves no room, the phase before it is shortened instead, and the reason is shown.
- **Game-ready floor, always on:** every week an athlete is playing or might play, they get an elastic primer, one max-velocity touch and one sport-intent exposure.

## D. When the schedule is unknown, play it safe
- Ask one question in the check-in: "When's your next game?" with four answers: this week, 2 to 3 weeks, more than a month, not sure.
- "Not sure" means in-season maintenance with the game-ready floor. No heavy offseason blocks are started on a guess.
- Re-ask once a week until the answer is known, and use the athlete's own history (last season's pattern, their level and age group) to estimate what is likely.

## E. Life changes live in the mandatory check-ins
Move these into the morning and night check-ins as one-tap chips, while keeping them in the inbox too: Travelling, Need a break, Something hurts, Games cancelled, Back to normal.
- The check-in asks plainly: "Anything change?" with "Nope" as the first, biggest option.
- Entering the same thing twice never creates two entries; the timeline dedupe rules apply.
- Anything entered here re-plans exactly as an inbox entry does.

## F. Explain the why, simply, fascia first
Every phase and every discipline's block carries a short "why this matters", two or three sentences, plain words, no jargon, readable by a young athlete.
- The Hammers lens is whole-chain and connective tissue first: the springy tissue that stores and returns energy, with muscle as the engine that drives it.
- Write it as how Hammers trains, never as proven science. No mechanism is stated as fact. Nothing becomes a medical claim.
- Examples:
  - **Power Potential:** "We build the tissue that stores and returns energy, and the strength behind it. It's what lets everything later hit harder without breaking you down."
  - **Power Building:** "Now we add force. Heavy work and fast bar speed teach your body to produce more power on demand."
  - **Explosiveness:** "We turn that force into quickness — short ground contacts, fast hands, snap. This is where power becomes speed."
  - **Game-Ready Production:** "We hold everything you built and keep you fresh, so the best version of you shows up on game day."

## G. Tests to add
- A weekend-only athlete never receives an offseason block, and the mid-week days are in-season training.
- A three-week postponement produces a mini block plus a 5-day ramp, and the ramp never overlaps the first game back.
- A six-week shutdown produces a full short arc plus a 10 to 14 day ramp.
- "Not sure" schedule keeps the athlete at in-season maintenance with the game-ready floor.
- Unity check: two disciplines never sit in different phases unless a hold is flagged.
- Every phase and block shows its "why", and no "why" text contains a medical or proven-science claim.
- Check-in chips create the same entries as the inbox, with no duplicates.
