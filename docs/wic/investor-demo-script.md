# Investor demo script

Nine demo accounts, all marked as test accounts: hidden from analytics,
rankings, leaderboards and scout search, with no recruiting consent on file.
Sign in with the e-mail below and "Forgot password" (every address is the
owner's own inbox with plus-addressing), or use the owner-only demo tool to
get a one-tap sign-in link.

Run each stop on a phone-width window. Total time: about 12 minutes.

## The walk-through, in order

**1. Open with the 13-year-old (account 1).**
Today's plan is already built and waiting — nobody waits for a page to think.
Open "Before you start": it names the phase the athlete is in, how many weeks
are left, what comes next and why this phase matters. Say the line: the plan
knows where he is in his year, not just what day it is.

**2. Show that the athlete talks back — "Tell Hammer" (same account).**
It sits on the front of the card. Eight big buttons, no typing, three taps at
most. Tap "Game added", pick this weekend, confirm. The next seven days
re-plan on the spot. Point out the undo: anything can be taken back within a
day.

**3. The high-school shortstop (account 2) — in-season and arm load.**
He has weekend games, so today's session drops the heavy lift and keeps the
primer. Open the throwing card: one tap logs a round of throws or catching
throw-downs, and the arm ledger adds up everything thrown today and this
week, not just pitches.

**4. The college outfielder (account 3) — the full arc.**
Off-season with a long runway. Show the phase strip: several phases planned
out, each with its own job. This is the part a college coach recognises.

**5. The professional (account 4) — 162 games.**
In-season maintenance, the post-game plan, and a card that never asks for
more than the day allows.

**6. Softball (accounts 5 and 6).**
The windmill pitcher has a tournament this weekend, so her throwing budget is
tighter all week. The catcher's throw-downs are counted the same way a
pitcher's pitches are. Say the line: softball is not baseball with a bigger
ball — the limits are its own.

**7. The two-way athlete (account 7).**
Pitcher and outfielder. One arm, one budget: everything he throws in either
role lands on the same ledger.

**8. The comeback pitcher (account 8) — the ramp.**
Back from a ten-day break, partway through a build-up. The plan will not let
him jump back to where he was; it steps him up.

**9. The 14-year-old pitcher (account 9) — the youth limits.**
Age-based daily pitch maximum, required rest days, and the arm total shown
next to it. Close on this one: this is the part parents care about.

## The live moment to do on stage

On the shortstop (account 2), open "Tell Hammer", tap "Game cancelled" and
pick the weekend game. Come back to Today. The week re-plans in front of the
room: the work that was being saved for the game moves back in.

## Staff view

The owner account has staff access to all nine. Open Staff View for the
14-year-old to show the coach-facing detail and the PDF export, including the
research note behind the pitcher-catcher budget, which athletes never see.
