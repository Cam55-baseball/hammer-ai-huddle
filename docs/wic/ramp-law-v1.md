# Ramp Law v1 — every discipline, every athlete
**Owner-directed, 2026-09-24.** Safety first, dominance as the goal. Nothing here is cookie-cutter: every number below is a starting point that the athlete's own history, age, maturity, pain record and tolerance adjust.

## 0. Scope
"Throwing" means ALL throwing athletes — position players and pitchers. Pitchers carry extra mound rules on top.
Ramps apply to: throwing, lifting, speed, bat speed, conditioning, jumps, and upper-body and hand or wrist work.

## 1. The general ramp law
D = days since the athlete's last meaningful exposure in that discipline.

| D (days off) | Ramp |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | none |
| 3–7 | 3 to 4 ramp days: reduced volume, intent about 60 → 80%, one variable changed at a time |
| 8–28 | ramp days = 2 × D, minimum 7 |
| 29 or more | the structured build in §2 or §3, not 2 × D |

Why the cap: doubling a 4-month layoff would mean 8 months of ramp. Published preseason throwing programs run 6 to 8 weeks even after a full offseason, so beyond 28 days off we switch from the doubling rule to a structured build with a ceiling.

**Every ramp, every discipline:**
- Change one variable at a time: distance, then volume, then intent. Never two in one step.
- At least 48 hours between steps.
- A step advances only if the last one was pain-free and next-day soreness stayed under threshold. A failed gate repeats the step; nothing skips ahead.
- Fatigue ends a session, not the clock.
- No ramp may be shortened to fit a calendar. The block before it shortens instead, and the athlete is told why.

## 2. Throwing — offseason break and build
**The break at the start of downtime**
- Owner rule: 4 days of no throwing per month of downtime, so a 4-month offseason opens with 16 no-throw days.
- Under 18 (Step 27): the ratio may rise to 5 to 8 no-throw days per month, chosen from the athlete's own signals (age and maturity, growth, pain in the last 90 days, last season's load, arm tank, prior break and ramp tolerance). Never a flat number. If break plus ramp won't fit, the break shortens; the ramp never does.
- Weekly scheduled non-throw days stay exactly as they are, and change only when pain, comfort or tolerance changes.

**The build back**
- Break of 3 to 7 days: 3 to 4 ramp days, catch play only, distance before intent.
- 8 to 28 days: ramp days = 2 × D, minimum 7. So 8 days off means a 16-day ramp.
- 29 days or more: structured build — 2 to 3 weeks of interval throwing (catch play) only, then add flat ground once a week, then mound work for pitchers once a week building to twice. Ceiling: 8 weeks for pitchers, 5 weeks for position players.
- Young athletes: no throwing on consecutive days early in the build. Older athletes: never three days in a row.
- Full intent only after full distance, pain-free.
- High-intent work (max bullpens, pulldowns, velocity days) unlocks only after the whole build plus two clean weeks.
- Pitchers finish the build comfortable with roughly 20 to 25 pitches off the mound before a game counts.
- Position players get the same care: distance ladder, then intent, then position-specific throws (infield quick release, outfield crow-hop, catcher pop times).

## 3. The other disciplines
| Discipline | 3–7 days off | 8–28 days off | 29+ days off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lifting | first session at the envelope floor | 2 × D, min 7; class capped at Moderate; no new methods or tiers | 14 to 21 days, capacity first, then strength, then speed-strength |
| Speed | 1 to 2 build-up days at 85–90% | 2 × D, min 7; distance before intent; 48h between max-velocity days | 14 days: acceleration, then max velocity, then change of direction |
| Bat speed | 2 days of reduced swing volume | 2 × D, min 7; dry swings → tee → front toss → live | 14 to 21 days; no overload or underload bats until two clean weeks |
| Conditioning | none | 2 × D, min 7; aerobic and tempo first | 14 days; repeat-sprint work last |
| Jumps and upper-body plyos | drop one tier for the first session | back one tier, earn it forward again | restart at Tier 1 or U1 and earn upward |

## 4. Personal adjustment (no cookie cutter)
Multiply the ramp length by the largest applicable factor, then round up:
- age 13 to 15: ×1.25
- Growth Mode: ×1.25
- pain in that area in the last 90 days: ×1.25
- first time in this discipline: ×1.25
- elite or professional with a clean history: ×0.9, but never below the discipline minimum

Also learned per athlete over time: how they tolerated previous ramps moves their factor within ±20%, at most 5% a week, versioned, auto-disabled if outcomes get worse.

## 5. Unity
A ramp is part of the athlete's single season state and phase. It appears in Hammer's Today, the before-you-start drawer and the weekly card, with its own plain line: "Throwing build, day 6 of 16 — distance first, intent later."

## 6. Tests
- 8 days off throwing produces a 16-day ramp; 2 days off produces none; 5 days off produces 3 to 4 ramp days.
- A 4-month offseason opens with at least 16 no-throw days, and more when the age rule is longer.
- A 120-day layoff produces a structured build of 6 to 8 weeks for a pitcher, never 240 days.
- A failed pain or soreness gate repeats the step.
- No young athlete gets consecutive throwing days early in a build; no athlete gets three in a row.
- High-intent throwing stays locked until the build plus two clean weeks.
- Every discipline's ramp appears in the day plan and the drawer, and never overlaps a game.
- A calendar squeeze shortens the previous block, never the ramp.
- Ramp length changes correctly with each personal factor.
