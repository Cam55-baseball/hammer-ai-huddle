# Hammers Intelligence — The General, Game IQ and the Owner's Coaching Console

## 1. The rule that governs everything: simple as sports

Every screen must pass the five-year-old test. A player opens The General and understands, without help:
- Where am I? (one grade, one band: Sub Par, Acceptable, Elite)
- What's working?
- What's holding me back?
- What do I do next? (one drill, one exercise, one habit, or one game plan)
- How will I know it worked?

Every piece of advice in Hammers uses the same Hammers Card, five short lines:
1. We see: "Your bat speed went up 2 mph."
2. Because: "You logged 9 of 10 lifts and your last 3 swing reports improved."
3. How sure: ●●● Measured · ●●○ Pattern · ●○○ Hunch · ○○○ Need more data
4. Do this: "Keep the Power Primer pairs. Add 2 heavy med-ball scoops this week."
5. Check back: "Re-test bat speed on your next testing day."

Measured = we recorded it. Pattern = it keeps showing up together. Hunch = it might be a cause; we'll test it. Need more data = we won't guess. Hammers never says one thing caused another unless it was measured.

## 2. The General: four tabs, the same for every subscription

TODAY
- Today's readiness as a small chip on the identity card. Tap it for a Hammers Card: why today looks this way, what we changed in today's plan, one thing to do for tomorrow.
- Today's main focus (from the Smart Week, recalculated every night with the plan).
- What changed since yesterday.

PERFORMANCE
- One row for every area in the player's subscription: hitting, pitching, throwing, defense, baserunning, speed, strength and power, mobility. Pitchers see pitching; hitters see hitting; nobody sees an empty section.
- Each row: grade and band, trend arrow; opens to what's working, what's holding you back, do this next (drills, exercises, videos from the Vault), check back.
- Tests live in the Vault. Performance shows the summary and trend with a "See tests" link. No duplicates.

GAME IQ
- Everything from game logging: stats, spray charts and heat maps (only from real tap-in data), situations, and the section 4 reports.

GROWTH
- The running total of the six-week recaps: what changed, what's unresolved, the next priority.
- Body weight over time, with the goal and rate of change.
- Sleep and eating habits, with the patterns they line up with (Pattern or Hunch, never "caused").
- Lifting and athleticism measurements against the player's own baseline, with "do this next".
- "What's holding you back": the one or two constraints that matter most to the goal, with evidence, how sure, and the test that would settle it.
- Health flags: only real, repeated pain or movement findings, always paired with "Please talk to a professional". No injury predictions.

CNS load and fascial load: before either is shown, prove what each actually calculates, from which inputs, and whether it's valid. Anything not defensible is shown as "training exposure" instead of a made-up body measurement.

## 3. The learning loop: every "Do this" gets checked
1. Hammers notices a pattern. 2. It explains the evidence and how sure it is. 3. It recommends one action. 4. It records whether the player did it. 5. It re-measures with a comparable test or game. 6. It marks the result: It worked / No change / Got worse, and adjusts.
This is how Hammers learns what helps each player, and what has helped other Hammers players with similar profiles, without exposing anyone's private data.

## 4. Game IQ: reports a pro would respect

Game logging becomes structured events: a 2-minute post-game entry, quick in-game taps (pitch location and ball in play), and video-tagged events. Every entry is labeled by source: player-entered, parent-entered, coach-verified, imported, video-tagged, or estimated. Nothing missing is ever filled with invented detail.

Hitter report: who you are as a hitter, strengths, what pitchers try against you, spray chart and zone heat map, results by count, pitch type and situation, chase and contact tendencies, how to grow each weakness into a strength.
Pitcher report: repertoire, velocity, location tendencies, results by count and batter side, sequences that work, two-strike execution, damage by pitch and location, what to build next.
Defense and throwing report: position-specific outcomes, throwing velocity and accuracy, exchange and release times where video can measure them, clean plays vs errors with fair denominators, footwork and transfer notes from video.
Baserunning report: leads, jumps, steal attempts and success, advancement decisions, home-to-first and steal times.
Mindset: routines, focus ratings and post-game reflections the player enters, compared with what happened. Hammers never guesses a player's mental state.
The matchup planner: hitter profile + pitcher profile + situation (count, outs, runners, score) → a game plan: what to sit on, what to watch for, how the pitcher will likely attack, what to do with two strikes. Always shows the sample size; small samples say "early read". Works both ways: a pitcher against a hitter, and a defense against a lineup.

## 5. The Owner's Coaching Console
A new owner-dashboard section where the owner teaches Hammers what he believes, per sport and area (results, mechanics, mindset, the mental game, game planning):
- Write a principle: plain text plus picks: sport, area, situation, when it applies, when it doesn't, connected drills or exercises.
- Attach evidence: experience, a study, or a Hammers pattern.
- Hammers uses it: principles guide the coaching engine's advice and game plans.
- Hammers tests it: each principle tracks whether players followed it and what happened next; coaches' "Do you agree?" votes feed in.
- It earns a badge: "Hammers belief" → "Seen in Hammers players" → "Proven at Hammers" once it repeats across enough players.
- Nothing is overwritten silently: versioned, can be turned off, and the owner can see which principle produced any advice.
