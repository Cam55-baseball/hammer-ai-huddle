1. **Lifting dropped the one easy-base-run in a conditioning session.** The load-spike trim could remove the required easy run and leave only a hard sprint, so the day was refused. The trim now keeps that one row at its lowest dose, just like it already did for required lifts.
2. **Busy game weeks put lifting on game days.** With 4+ games in a week the builder still added lifting on game days, but the safety check never allows that, so the whole game day was refused. Lifting now stays on non-game days only. That only makes days lighter.
3. **Sunday games were refused.** A Sunday game day has no lifting or conditioning, so the day had no physical rows and the check failed it as "empty". A game day may now have no physical rows. The game card is the day. Any other empty day is still refused.

## What this shows, in plain words
- **Pitching is its own card** every training day for Complete Pitcher and Golden 2Way: bullpens (2 a week), flush the day after, long toss, fielding-only, touch, and rest on Sunday. 5Tool never gets pitching.
- **Complete Pitcher gets no hitting, bunting or baserunning.** Defense is pitcher fielding on 6 days.
- **Bunting is 2 days a week** inside the Hitting card for 5Tool and Golden 2Way, never on Sunday, never for pitchers. The one short week: the pro 5Tool's 5-game week has 1 bunting day (most hitting days were game days).
- **Game days** have their own "Game day — play and log your game" card, a pregame defense primer, no lifting, no speed and no conditioning, then in-season recovery.
- **Sunday** is rest: soft recovery only, all training cards show "off today".
- **Off-season vs in-season:** off-season strength is "off-season volume" 4 days a week. In-season it's "in-season potentiation" 2 days a week. Speed goes from "off-season volume" to "in-season freshness". Fueling switches to "game-day carb-forward".
- **Power Primer** has real numbers on heavy-lift days. Example: "Trap-bar deadlift 1–3 reps, then 2-hand overhead med-ball throw 3–5 reps (3 lb), rest 60–90 s". Max-effort throws stay off (no real-ball throws).
